# Riso — style pack

Hand-pulled risograph print: flat spot-color inks stamped through a screen,
visible ink grain, and a deliberate slight misregistration between ink layers
on uncoated paper. Two or three inks do all the work — shapes separate by ink
color and overlap darkens, never by shading. Loud, tactile, a little imperfect;
the print IS the look.

## Prompt blocks (replace the template's LINE LANGUAGE and STYLE lines)

```text
LINE LANGUAGE: draw EVERYTHING — mascot, props, environment — as flat shapes
built from ONE even-weight rounded outline; fills are single flat spot-color
stamps with visible ink grain (fine stipple noise inside every filled shape,
lighter at edges); NO gradients, NO hatching, NO painterly blending — where
two ink colors overlap they multiply into a darker third tone.

STYLE: RISOGRAPH PRINT on uncoated paper — flat spot-color inks applied like
a duplicator press: slight paper-drift misregistration between color layers
(outline and fill sit a hair apart), ink grain and roller streaks inside
fills, soft ink bleed at shape edges; two or three inks total; warm paper
tooth visible in every empty area; NOT digital-clean, NOT vector-crisp, NOT
3D, no photorealism, no smooth shading.
```

## Palette mapping

- **Paper ground** ← the palette paper, uncoated and warm; grain shows
  everywhere.
- **Structure ink** ← the palette structure hue as a flat stamped ink: every
  outline, annotation, and dark shape.
- **Accent** ← the palette accent as the second ink, reserved for the
  character's one focal accent part plus at most 1–2 tiny scene elements.
  Overlaps between the two inks multiply darker — use deliberately, max one
  overlap zone per image.

Classic default (no palette given): paper `#f5efe0`, structure ink riso blue
`#0078bf`, accent ink fluorescent pink `#ff48b0`.

## Character treatment

The mascot is printed with the same two-ink economy as the scene: flat fills
with grain, one outline color, and exactly ONE focal accent part in the accent
ink. Faces stay exactly locked. Misregistration may offset a fill from its
outline by a hair everywhere EXCEPT the face — the face registers true, so
the lock survives the print.

## QA deltas

- Fills must carry ink grain — a dead-flat digital fill is out of style.
- Misregistration is a hair's offset, never a doubled or ghosted image.
- More than two inks on a body, or accent ink spreading past the accent part,
  is a re-roll.
- The locked face registers true — a drifted face is a re-roll even if the
  rest of the print is charming.
